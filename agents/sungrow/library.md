## What a battery energy storage system (BESS) is
Keywords: bess, b e s s, battery energy storage, energy storage system, ess, storage, battery storage, store electricity, how storage works, bms, pcs, ems, round trip, duration, peak shaving, frequency
- A BESS stores electricity in batteries and releases it later: solar from the day used at night, power stored when it's cheap and used at peak times, backup during power cuts, and fast support to keep the grid stable.
- Main parts: battery cells (today almost always LFP, lithium iron phosphate), a battery management system (BMS) that watches every cell, a power conversion system (PCS) that charges and discharges, an energy management system (EMS) that decides when, plus cooling and fire protection.
- Capacity is energy in kWh or MWh. Power is how fast it delivers, in kW or MW. Duration is capacity divided by power: a 4 MWh, 1 MW system runs 4 hours.
- Round-trip efficiency is the share of energy you get back, about 85 to 95% for lithium systems. Cycle life is how many full charges it lasts.

- Most inverters are grid-following: they copy the grid's voltage and frequency. Grid-forming inverters create their own stable voltage and frequency, like a power station's generator, so they can steady weak grids, provide inertia, and restart a grid after a blackout (black start).

## Battery chemistry: LFP, NMC, lead-acid, sodium-ion
Keywords: lfp, lithium iron phosphate, lifepo4, nmc, lithium ion, lead acid, tubular battery, sodium ion, chemistry, cycle life, thermal runaway, battery type, safest battery, battery life
- LFP (lithium iron phosphate) is the safest common lithium chemistry and lasts a long time, typically 6,000 to over 10,000 cycles. Sungrow's storage uses LFP.
- NMC lithium packs more energy into less space (used in many electric cars) but is less heat-tolerant.
- Lead-acid (including tubular batteries) is cheap, but lasts far fewer cycles, should only be drained about halfway, and needs topping up with distilled water.
- Sodium-ion is a newer, low-cost chemistry with lower energy density, just starting to reach the market.
- Thermal runaway is a cell overheating uncontrollably. Good systems prevent it with a BMS, cooling, and barriers between cells. Keep batteries shaded and ventilated, which matters in Tamil Nadu's heat.

## PID Zero and PID recovery
Keywords: pid, potential induced degradation, pid zero, pid recovery, panel degradation, power loss, output dropping, humid, coastal
- PID (potential-induced degradation) is a power loss in panels caused by a voltage difference between the cells and the grounded frame. It is worse in hot, humid, and coastal climates, and can cut output badly over the years.

## How solar power works
Keywords: how solar works, how does solar work, photovoltaic, pv, sunlight, electricity, dc, ac, basics, beginner, explain solar
- Solar panels are made of silicon cells. Sunlight knocks electrons loose in the cells, producing direct current (DC).
- The inverter turns DC into alternating current (AC) at the grid's voltage and frequency, so it can run home appliances. Power not used at that moment flows to the grid through the net meter, or charges a battery.
- Output follows the sun: it starts after sunrise, peaks around noon, and stops at night. On-grid systems switch off during a power cut so they don't feed a line being repaired.

## Solar panel technologies
Keywords: panel, panels, module, modules, topcon, perc, hjt, heterojunction, bifacial, back contact, bc, mono, monocrystalline, poly, polycrystalline, n-type, p-type, efficiency, wattage, watt, which panel, best panel, half cut, glass glass, temperature coefficient
- Mono PERC (p-type) panels were the standard, at about 21% efficiency. TOPCon (n-type) panels, about 22 to 23%, are now the mainstream. Heterojunction (HJT) panels handle heat best. Back-contact (BC) panels put all the wiring behind the cell and are among the most efficient, about 23 to 24%. Old polycrystalline panels are less efficient.
- Bifacial panels also collect light on their back, adding about 5 to 15% on light-coloured roofs or raised structures.
- In hot places like Tamil Nadu, the temperature coefficient matters: n-type TOPCon and HJT lose less output in heat than PERC.
- Panels sold today are typically 540 to 620 W, with 25 to 30-year performance warranties. Sungrow doesn't make panels; its inverters work with all major panel brands.

## Inverter types
Keywords: inverter types, types of inverter, string inverter, central inverter, micro inverter, microinverter, off grid inverter, solar inverter vs home ups, difference between inverters, which inverter, single phase vs three phase
- String inverters take one or more strings of panels and suit most homes and businesses. Most Sungrow SG, SH, and MG models are string inverters.
- Central and modular inverters (like Sungrow's 1+X 2.0) serve large solar plants, in megawatt blocks.
- Microinverters sit behind each panel. They are rare in India because of cost.
- Inverters typically last 10 to 15 years or more; panels last 25 years or more.
- Hybrid inverters manage panels, a battery, and the grid together. Off-grid inverters run without the grid. A normal home UPS inverter only charges a battery from the grid and is not a solar inverter.
- Single-phase inverters suit single-phase connections. Three-phase inverters suit three-phase connections and bigger loads.

## On-grid, off-grid, and hybrid systems
Keywords: on grid, ongrid, off grid, offgrid, hybrid, grid tied, which system, difference, backup, power cut, diesel, generator, dg, village, farm
- On-grid: the cheapest per unit, uses net metering, and gives no backup during cuts. Best where power cuts are rare.
- Hybrid: grid plus battery, with net metering and backup. Best where cuts are common. Sungrow SH and MG hybrids switch in 0 to 10 milliseconds.
- Off-grid: no grid connection, everything comes from panels and batteries. It needs a bigger battery and costs more. Used for remote farms and sites.
- Generators: the MG20TL and MG25TL can connect a diesel generator; business sites can combine solar with a generator using a controller.

## Sizing a home system
Keywords: size, sizing, how many kw, what size, how big, capacity, bill, units per month, monthly units, consumption, roof area, square feet, sq ft, space, tilt, angle, direction, south facing, shadow, shade
- Rule of thumb: each kW of panels makes about 120 units a month in Tamil Nadu. Monthly units divided by 120 gives the kW needed. For example, 300 units needs about 2.5 kW, and 600 units about 5 kW.
- Guideline from the state subsidy guidance: up to 150 units a month, 1 to 2 kW; about 300 units, 2 to 3 kW; above 300 units, more than 3 kW.
- Roof space: about 80 to 100 square feet of shadow-free roof per kW.
- In Tamil Nadu panels face south, tilted about 10 to 15 degrees. Avoid shade from water tanks, walls, and trees, and plan for future loads like an electric car or more ACs.

## Generation, seasons, and performance
Keywords: generation, units per day, how much power, output, production, performance, low generation, less units, monsoon, northeast monsoon, summer, winter, cloudy, rain, season, performance ratio
- A well-installed system in Tamil Nadu makes about 4 units a day per kW on average, roughly 1,400 to 1,500 units a year per kW. A 5 kW system makes about 20 units a day.
- Tamil Nadu gets most of its rain from the northeast monsoon, October to December. Expect roughly 25 to 40% less generation in those months than in the sunny months, and much less on days of heavy rain. March to May has the strongest sun, though heat trims efficiency a little.
- Normal losses come from heat, dust, wiring, and the inverter. A good system delivers about 75 to 80% of the panels' rated output (its performance ratio).
- If generation drops suddenly: check the app for errors, clean the panels, and check for new shade or a tripped breaker.

## Net metering and the TNPDCL process in Tamil Nadu
Keywords: net meter, net metering, net metre, tnpdcl, tangedco, tneb, eb, electricity board, discom, bidirectional meter, two way meter, export, import, gross metering, net billing, application, portal, usrp, approval, feasibility, how long, inspection
- In Tamil Nadu, rooftop solar connections and net meters are handled by TNPDCL, the state's distribution company (formerly TANGEDCO). Applications go through the TNPDCL Unified Solar Rooftop Portal at tnebltd.gov.in, for subsidy and non-subsidy systems.
- Net metering: a two-way meter records units taken from and sent to the grid, and the bill is for the difference. Gross metering sells all solar output to the grid at a set tariff. Net billing values exported units at a different rate from imported ones.
- Steps: apply and get technical feasibility, install with an approved vendor, submit the work completion report, then TNPDCL inspects and fits the net meter. Guides report it often takes two to four months; the Sunbridger team can confirm current timelines.
- Guides cite a 10 kW cap for homes under net metering. Export is credited under the Tamil Nadu Electricity Regulatory Commission's rules.

## Subsidies for home rooftop solar (central and Tamil Nadu)
Keywords: subsidy, subsidies, pm surya ghar, surya ghar, muft bijli, free electricity, government scheme, tamil nadu subsidy, state subsidy, 78000, 1 lakh, how to apply subsidy, eligibility, eligible, dbt, bank account
- Central PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana: 30,000 rupees per kW for the first 2 kW and 18,000 rupees for the third kW, up to 78,000 rupees for 3 kW or more. It's for homes with grid-connected rooftop solar and net metering, using made-in-India (DCR) panels from an approved vendor. Off-grid systems and businesses don't qualify. Housing societies get a separate per-kW subsidy for common areas.
- Tamil Nadu's 2026-27 budget added a state rooftop subsidy of up to 1 lakh rupees for homes, on top of the central subsidy, also for made-in-India panels and approved installers. Its current status and exact amounts are something the Sunbridger team should confirm.
- Steps: register on the PM Surya Ghar portal, choose Tamil Nadu and TNPDCL, enter the electricity service number, get feasibility, install with an approved vendor, submit the completion report, and after inspection and net-meter fitting the subsidy goes to the bank account.

## ALMM and DCR panel rules
Keywords: almm, a l m m, dcr, domestic content, made in india panel, indian panel, list ii, list 2, approved list, mnre list, chinese panels, imported panels, cells
- ALMM List-I is the government's approved list of solar panel models; List-II, in force from 1 June 2026, approves the solar cells inside them.
- Subsidized, net-metered, and government-linked rooftop projects commissioned from 1 June 2026 need panels on List-I with cells on List-II.
- DCR (domestic content requirement) panels use Indian-made cells and come with a DCR certificate, which the PM Surya Ghar subsidy requires. Imported panels can't get one. One estimate says the cell rule adds up to about 3,000 rupees per kW.
- The ALMM lists cover panels and cells. For inverter requirements in subsidized systems, the Sunbridger team can confirm.

- Tamil Nadu is one of India's leading solar states, with roughly 10 GW or more of solar by 2026, and it added 2.4 GW in the first half of 2026, about 9% of India's additions. India as a whole had about 171 GW of solar by September 2026, including about 33 GW on rooftops.

## Cleaning and maintenance
Keywords: cleaning, clean, wash, maintenance, maintain, dust, water, upkeep, servicing, annual service, birds, pigeon, borewell water, hard water, brush
- Rinse panels with plain water every week or two in dusty areas, in the early morning or evening when they're cool. Use a soft brush or mop. Never use detergent, hard brushes, or high-pressure jets.
- Avoid hard borewell water, which leaves white scale. Use softened or RO water if you can.
- Watch generation in the iSolarCloud app; a sudden drop is a sign to check.
- Once a year: tighten connections, check cables for damage from rodents or sun, test the earthing and lightning arrester, and check the inverter's air vents and filters. Bird nets stop pigeons nesting under panels.

## Earthing, lightning protection, and electrical safety
Keywords: earthing, earth, grounding, earth pit, lightning, lightning arrester, la, spd, surge, mcb, rccb, isolator, cable, wire, mc4, connector, wiring, electric shock, fire
- A safe rooftop system has separate earth pits for the panel frames, the inverter, and the lightning arrester, plus surge protection (SPD) on the DC and AC sides. Sungrow inverters include Type II surge protection.
- Use proper DC solar cable (usually 4 or 6 square mm), matching MC4 connectors from one brand, a DC isolator, and AC breakers (MCB, and an RCCB for leakage).
- Loose or mismatched connectors are a common cause of heat and arcing; AFCI in Sungrow inverters adds protection.
- Electrical work should only be done by trained installers.

## Savings and payback
Keywords: payback, payback period, roi, return, return on investment, savings, save money, bill reduction, reduce bill, worth it, profitable, loan, emi, finance, financing, cost benefit
- Many homes in India recover the cost in about 4 to 6 years, depending on the tariff, how much solar power is used at home, and the subsidy. Panels then keep producing for 25 years or more.
- Each kW makes about 120 units a month, so savings per month are roughly 120 units times your tariff per unit.
- Businesses pay higher tariffs, so solar often pays back faster for shops, schools, and factories.

## Solar and energy terms explained
Keywords: meaning, what does it mean, full form, stands for, glossary, term, terms, abbreviation, kwp, kwh, unit, soc, dod, c-rate, lcoe, lcos, cuf, vpp, hems, bms, pcs, ems
- kW is power, kWp the peak rating of panels, and kWh (one unit) is energy. MW and MWh are a thousand times bigger, and GWh a million times.
- SOC is a battery's state of charge. DoD is depth of discharge, the share of capacity used. C-rate is how fast a battery charges or discharges compared with its size.
- BMS is the battery management system, PCS the power conversion system, EMS the energy management system, and HEMS a home energy management system.
- LCOE and LCOS are the lifetime cost per unit of solar energy and of stored energy. CUF (capacity utilization factor) is the share of the year's maximum possible output a plant actually produces, about 17 to 20% for solar in India.
- A VPP (virtual power plant) links many home batteries so they act together for the grid.
